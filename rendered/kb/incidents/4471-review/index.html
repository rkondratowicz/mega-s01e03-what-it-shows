Decision for this review

Keep the parser and the booking-form hint as separate problems, each with its own engineering ticket. The export’s incident narrative says “one issue, one fix queued”, but the running classification and tenant directory show two causes. A known label describes the desk’s classification; it is not evidence that every report has the same cause, or that a fix shipped.

Engineering outcome · 5 October

After the course accepted the initial split, the challenge page published a new engineering update: #4471 closed 05 oct. It records two different outcomes:

  • Parser — fixed: fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. The parser ticket is closed as completed, based on that desk update.
  • Hint — won’t fix: booking-v3 form retired. The hint ticket is closed as not planned, because the affected form was retired, not because its misleading hint was fixed.

The original report counts remain the historical scope of each problem. We did not execute a production regression test: the closure evidence is the course’s engineering update, not an independent deployment check. A closed parent ticket does not mean every contained problem was fixed.

Counts and symptoms come from the 4 October 2026 export, not a live view. Closure comes from the later engineering update above. The original incident page and the accepted 2024 decision are historical evidence, not the current rule recommended by this review.

Median receipt-to-merge delay: 0.2 days. These are desk processing times, not repair times or a measure of how long a customer was affected.

The counts use one report per ID, filter mergedInto === 4471, and call the supplied groupByCause / causeOf logic with tenantOf. The tenant’s locale and deployed booking form decide the cause. meta.browserLocale and meta.intakeTag are intake observations, not the rule. The two sets are disjoint and account for every unique report in the ticket.

Parser: month-first interpretation of day-first dates

Parser ticket. The shared parser treats date text as month/day for tenants whose locale puts the day first. For example, R-41203 reports 2 December becoming 12 February. R-41205 reports 3 October becoming 10 March despite intake tagging it hint from an en-US browser.

For regression review, engineering should use an explicit date contract at the parsing boundary, validate impossible dates, and test day-first and month-first input plus app, email and form paths. Do not silently change the form hint and call this fixed: it is a separate path.

Hint: booking-v3 prints the wrong date order

Hint ticket. For a day-first tenant, booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but the form reads the entered date in the tenant’s day-first order. A customer who follows that hint therefore gets the wrong date. The affected form tenants are Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour. Their app and email reports still follow the parser rule; tenant name alone is not enough.

For example, R-41241 is an Alder Physio form report: 6 August became 8 June. If this form were retained, the hint would need to match its accepted locale order, with tests for booking-v2 and booking-v3. Verify both the displayed hint and the date submitted. A shared-parser fix alone does not establish that this misleading instruction is repaired.

What remains uncertain

The desk flagged R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344 and R-41620 as one-day moves. Their bodies include a one-day shift, an invite arriving before booking, and different days in Lisbon and Toronto. The supplied rule still files all of them under parser. Keep them there for this reconciliation, but retain the IDs and request original timestamps, time zones and raw inputs before diagnosing a third bug. Do not invent a timezone fix from the wording.

The queue kept moving

Reports arrived in April, May and June before the first merges on 2 July. A July merge spike is partly backlog processing, not a July onset. Receipt counts continue into October; marking the ticket known did not stop reports. Neither series proves when a fix deployed, how many users were affected, or whether the incident is over. Inspect new arrivals against their tenants and form versions before moving them; rerun the counts when the export changes.
